Arthur Kiron played the central role in encouraging me to donate my photographic archive to the University of Pennsylvania. The gift was made without financial compensation, based on the understanding that the photographs would be preserved, cataloged, and made available for future generations—insofar as anything can truly be preserved "in perpetuity."

The word perpetuity itself deserves reflection. Nothing lasts forever, particularly in a world that often seems increasingly uncertain. Yet the hope behind the gift was simple: that these photographs, documenting the lives of others over more than six decades, would remain accessible to students, scholars, artists, and the public long after I am gone.

That aspiration—to preserve memory rather than to seek financial reward—was the reason for making the gift.

— Laurence Salzmann
